#   Player Registration

All Players playing in the Gaming League must be registered before they can play a game.
However **Players can registers after a season starts**.

##  Minimum Registration

The minimum registration requirements are...
- The Player's Name
- Contact details (so that other Player's can arrange a game)
- Club Membership Number if applicable

## Registered Games

Every player must register the games they are willing and able or interested in playing. 
These will be the games that they will be able to play in the league and the list from which an opponent must select a game for an agreed fixture.

For each registered game the player should provide...
1. Game name.
2. Rules edition or version, where applicable - if no version is provided then it is assumed that the player is willing to play any version of that game.
3. Estimated number of times the player has played the game - if not provided then it is assumed that the Player is an Intermediate (average) level player of that game.

Initially registering games is not mandatory and Players may also register additional games during the season.

Once a game has been selected for an agreed fixture, it cannot be removed from the player's registration until that fixture has been completed.

##  Player Proficiency Ratings

Proficiency Ratings are used to assess a player's familiarity with the rules of a particular game. 
The ratings are as follows:

| Level | Rating       | Description                                                                                                       | 
|-------|--------------|-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| 1     | Novice       | Not played the game but interested in learning                                                                    | 
| 2     | Beginner     | Player has basic knowledge of the rules and how to play                                                           | 
| 3     | Intermediate | Player has good knowledge of the game                                                                             | 
| 4     | Advanced     | Player has extensive knowledge of the game and most of its rules.                                                 | 
| 5     | Expert       | Player is highly skilled in the game and its tactics and willing to teach the game to Novice and Beginner players | 

The point about the Proficiency Rating is not about "differences in ability" but "differences in familiarity" with the rules of a game.
Some (most) games need to be played multiple times before someone becomes expert in the rules (even if they are still rubbish at the game itself) so this intends to capture that somehow.

>    Design Note: Board Games tend to have less of a learning curve that War Games so the number of games for a Proficiency Rating can be lower depending on type of game.
>    We could even refine further using the Board Games Geek Difficulty Rating.

The Proficiency Rating is based on the number of times the Player has played a game and the complexity of that game as defined on sites such as Board Games Geek.

The league organizer may review ratings where there is a reasonable concern that they are inaccurate.

The initial Proficiency Ratings are subsequently updated as games are played and players improve their skills in each game.

### Board Games

For Board Games the Proficiency Rating is based on the number of times the Player has played a game and the complexity of that game as defined on sites such as Board Games Geek.
Board Games Geek classifies games using a numeric range between Lightweight (1) through Mediumweight (3) up to Heavyweight (5). This is simplified into Simple (BGG <2), Intermediate (BGG 2 or 3) and Complex (BGG >4).

| Level | Rating       | Simple  | Intermediate | Complex  |
|-------|--------------|---------|--------------|----------|
| 1     | Novice       | 0 games | 0 games      | 0 games  |
| 2     | Beginner     | 1       | 1 to 3       | 1 to 5   |
| 3     | Intermediate | 2 to 4  | 4 to 8       | 6 to 12  |
| 4     | Advanced     | 5 to 10 | 9 to 14      | 13 to 20 |
| 5     | Expert       | 10+     | 15+          | 20+      |

>   Design Note: This is an arbitrary set of values and may be subject to refinement  

### Miniatures Game

>   Unlike Board Games, there is no generally accepted complexity rating for Miniatures Games and no single web-site where details of a game may be recorded. So we need to make one up.

Although we tend to think of Miniatures Games in terms of the "scale" or "scope" of the battle fought between 2 or more players the actual complexity in a Miniatures Game tends to be reflected in...
1. The number of different units types that a player can choose from when constructing their army
2. The number of pre-defined factions that a player can choose from where each faction is unique with their own "racial" capabilities, advantages and disadvantages.

Consequently, the complexity of a Miniatures Game can be estimated from the product of the number of different unit types and the number of defined factions in the game.

    Complexity = Number of Unit Types * Number of Factions

| Rating       | Complexity | Example                     |
|--------------|------------|-----------------------------|
| Simple       | 0 to 10    | Lion Rampant, Saga, Pillage |
| Intermediate | 11 to 100  |                             |
| Complex      | over 100   | Bolt Action, Kings of War   |

The Proficiency Level for each Complexity Level is then... 

| Level | Rating       | Simple  | Intermediate | Complex  |
|-------|--------------|---------|--------------|----------|
| 1     | Novice       | 0 games | 0 games      | 0 games  |
| 2     | Beginner     | 1       | 1 to 3       | 1 to 5   |
| 3     | Intermediate | 2 to 4  | 4 to 8       | 6 to 12  |
| 4     | Advanced     | 5 to 10 | 9 to 14      | 13 to 20 |
| 5     | Expert       | 10+     | 15+          | 20+      |

>   Design Note: This is an arbitrary set of values and may be subject to refinement
